# Content and voice

## Positioning

Laura Benavente — Senior Product Designer, complex B2B SaaS systems and AI-native experiences. Madrid, working remotely with international teams.

The career arc, which is the differentiator and should never be buried: **accounting → product design → strategic design → AI-native experiences.** She worked in accounting at a Madrid law and economics firm from 2008 to 2010 — journal entries, receivables and payables, bank reconciliations — and a decade later was designing software for accounting and tax firms. Almost no product designer can say that.

Employers: frog (Feb 2019 – Jun 2021, Telefónica) and Wolters Kluwer (Jun 2021 – Jul 2026, B2B SaaS for accounting and tax firms, remote and international).

## Case study structure

Every case follows the same spine. Two sections carry the weight; the rest is scaffolding.

1. **The context** — what the situation was, in plain terms
2. **The challenge** — ⭐ one memorable sentence, given its own full screen. This is what a recruiter repeats to a colleague.
3. **My role** — what Laura decided, not what the team did
4. **The approach** — the steps, numbered
5. **What I chose not to build** — ⭐ where judgement shows. Deferred is not the same as discarded, and saying which is the point.
6. **The first step** — what actually shipped
7. **Outcome** — what it produced, without invented metrics
8. **What I took from it** — three things, including one that was wrong

### The two starred sections are mandatory

A case without a single memorable challenge sentence is a list of activities. A case without an admitted misjudgement is not credible to anyone senior. Both have to be there.

## Voice

- **First person for decisions.** "I proposed", "I narrowed the scope", "I defended it in stakeholder reviews". Team actions stay plural and honest: "we ran concept validation with three customers".
- **Name the trade-off.** Every scope decision had a cost. Say what it was.
- **State limits precisely.** "Three customers is a directional signal, not statistical evidence" is stronger than implying it was research at scale.
- **No adjectives doing argumentative work.** Not "a powerful, intuitive experience" — say what it did and let the reader conclude.
- **No metric that cannot be sourced.** If Laura did not give a number, there is no number.

## Confidentiality wording

The employer may be named. The product may not.

> Developed for **Wolters Kluwer** · B2B SaaS for accounting and tax firms. Under confidentiality, original screens and identifying product details cannot be shown. Everything here is reconstructed at concept level. For this case study, let's call the product **Tax Cloud**.

Anonymised product names in use: **Tax Cloud** (cloud ecosystem case), **Practice Suite** (AI document drafting case). Movistar, Telefónica and frog may be named — that work is already public on her CV and LinkedIn — but the screens are still reconstructed.

Every image caption states its status: *Reconstructed — client identities withheld*, *Reconstructed wireframe — no original UI, no real data*, or *Reconstructed — the tension, not the interface*.

## The three cases

| | Subject | The memorable sentence |
|---|---|---|
| **Story 1** | Cloud ecosystem for tax professionals, Wolters Kluwer | *The hardest thing to move to the cloud was not the software. It was knowing which client you are working on.* |
| **Story 2** | AI-assisted document drafting, released in Canada | *Our users were already writing with AI. They were just doing it outside our product.* |
| **Story 3** | Smart WiFi Selfcare, Movistar at frog, 2019 | *The diagnostic test was slow, and no amount of design was going to make it faster.* |

Story 2 is the one that positions Laura for AI Product Designer roles. Its strongest fact: **the AI was not in the product requirement — she proposed it, and justified it with research the company already had.** That is product authorship, not execution.
